K-S Stencil.

Engineered letters with the joints showing — and now a real object. One geometry drives both the flat mark and the 3D piece, so they can never drift apart.

DRAG TO TURN · 3D

The construction

every cut has a reason

Drawn on a 64-unit grid. The K's arms stand apart from the stem by a stencil gap; the S is two exact bowls that meet at the spine with a bridge cut — where a real stencil would need one. The orange block sits on the grid's centre line, the one colour in the mark.

Grid
64 × 64 units; letters 34 units tall, centred
Stem
6.6 units — the heaviest stroke, the K's spine
Stencil gap
2.6 units between the stem and both arms
Arms
Parallel to each other at 63°, the same weight as the stem
S bowls
Two arcs, outer radius 10.1, stroke 6.4 — geometric, not calligraphic
Spine cut
8° on each side of the spine: the bridge that makes it a stencil
The block
5.2 units square, International Orange #FF5B1F

At every size

down to a browser tab

The flat mark is what the site uses everywhere; at small sizes it switches to a heavier cut (wider gaps, a larger block) so the stencil still reads at 16 px.
